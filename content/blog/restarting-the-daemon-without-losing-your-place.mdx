I update tuios a lot, as you might guess. Several times a day on some days.
And every update had the same annoying ending. The new binary is on disk, but
the daemon that holds all my panes is still the old one. To move it, I had to
detach, run `tuios kill-server`, start tuios again, and then go to every other
terminal that was attached and attach it again by hand. Nothing on the way
told me what the kill was about to stop.

That's fixed now. Here is the whole thing from inside tuios, with an agent, a
web server and a shell open:

## Say what stops, first

The first piece is a command, `tuios restart-server`. Before it does
anything, it shows a plan: the version running now, the version you get after,
which binary that comes from, every program that will stop, and what comes
back. Then it asks.

The programs in your panes do stop. That's the honest part of the plan. What
comes back is each session with its layout, its pane history and fresh shells
in the same folders, from the session files tuios
[saves and now reads back](/blog/sessions-come-back). An agent that can resume
its conversation is named in the plan, and offered back after the restart.

The app shows the same plan. When the daemon runs an older build than the
client, the dock says so, and a click (or the jump key) opens the dialog from
the clip. It's also in the palette and in the settings. The dialog and the CLI
share their words from one package, so they never disagree about what a
restart does.

There's one more check between the dialog and the restart. The dialog sends a
digest of the plan you agreed to. If a program started or stopped in the
seconds since, the restart refuses with "What a restart stops changed after
you agreed to it." and nothing changes. You only ever agree to the list you
saw.

## Which tuios?

"Restart on the new build" sounds simple until you ask which file is the new
build. My first thought was `/proc/self/exe`, the binary that's running. That's
exactly wrong after an update. The updater replaces the file at the path, and
`/proc/self/exe` still points at the old inode, so you'd restart on the
version you were trying to leave.

So `restart-server` looks at two candidates: its own path, which `tuios update`
replaces in place, and the `tuios` on your `PATH`, which Nix, mise, asdf and
Linuxbrew like to move around. It runs `--version` on both and takes the
newer. If that's the same version as now, the plan says "This restart starts
the same version.", so you're never surprised.

## Killing the pane you're typing in

The fun edge: you run `tuios restart-server` in a tuios pane. Stopping the
daemon kills that pane, which kills the shell, which kills `restart-server`
halfway, after the stop and before the start. Now you have no daemon at all.

The stop and the start now run in a small worker in a process session of its
own, with SIGHUP ignored. The command you typed can die with its pane, and the
worker still starts the new daemon. Each step goes to a `restart.log` beside
the daemon log, and `tuios restart-server --last` tells you how the last one
went. An e2e test does exactly this: it runs `restart-server --yes` inside a
pane of the daemon it restarts, and a daemon has to hold the session again
afterwards.

A lock beside the socket lets only one restart run at a time. The second one
says "Another restart of the daemon is running." instead of racing.

## Coming back, in the same process

When you restart from the app, the client doesn't quit and ask you to start
it again. On Unix it execs the new build in place, as
`attach <session> --after-restart`, so it keeps its pid and its terminal. It
carries its flags along, `--debug` and `--server` included. The e2e test
checks that the same pid is attached to the same session afterwards, on a
daemon of the newer build, with both panes, their names and their history.

## The other clients

That covered the client that asked for the restart. Every other client
attached to the same daemon still quit with "The connection to the TUIOS
daemon was lost", and a client couldn't tell a restart from a stop or a crash
anyway. They all look like a closed socket.

So [#891](https://github.com/Gaurav-Gosain/tuios/pull/891) gave the daemon a
goodbye. Before it closes, it tells each client why:

| The daemon | The client |
| --- | --- |
| says "restart" | waits up to 15 s for the new daemon, then reattaches |
| says "stop" (plain `kill-server`) | quits at once, as before |
| says nothing (a crash, or a daemon older than this) | waits up to 6 s, in case one comes back |

Each goodbye write has a 500 ms deadline, so a client that isn't reading can
hold the shutdown back by half a second at most. While a client waits, it dials
every 500 ms, the dock says "Waiting for the daemon. Keys are not sent.", and
an attached client runs no timer for any of this.

Keys typed during the wait are dropped on purpose, not held. After a restart
the panes are new shells, so a held key could land in a different program
than the one you were typing to. I'd rather lose a keystroke than send `rm` to
the wrong place.

## What review found after it landed

The final review of the stack found three real things, fixed in
[#934](https://github.com/Gaurav-Gosain/tuios/pull/934):

- A waiting client capped its handshake at 2 s, then read the attach reply with
  the usual 30 s deadline. A daemon that said hello and then stalled kept the
  client waiting about 32 s, while the docs promised 15. The attach reply is
  now bound by the end of the wait.
- `tuios hosts sync --restart` stopped remote daemons with a plain
  `kill-server`, so their clients heard "stop" and quit instead of
  reattaching. The scripts now pass `--restart`, and fall back to the plain
  stop for a binary older than the flag.
- After a reattach, the agents view's live box for an agent on another
  workspace could stop updating until you pressed a key.

The three feature PRs carry 15 negative controls on top of their e2e tests,
each one a piece of the restart cut out on purpose to check that a test
fails. One of them runs the steps in the command itself instead of the
worker, and no daemon comes back, which is the pane case from above.

## What it cost

The three feature PRs added 57,344, 57,344 and 24,576 bytes on linux/amd64.

## What it does not do yet

A restart still stops the programs in your panes. A long build or an agent in
the middle of a turn ends, and comes back as a fresh shell with its history.
Swapping the daemon binary while the pane programs keep running is the bigger
goal, and its first PR is in review. Until then, `tuios update` followed by
`tuios restart-server` is two commands, it tells you exactly what it's about to
do, and nobody has to attach anything by hand :)

The [restart page](/docs/daemon-restart) has every flag.
