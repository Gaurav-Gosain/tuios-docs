`tuios.dev/loom` draws [TML](/docs/libraries/tml) documents. You give it a document and a size. It gives you a cell buffer, which renders to ANSI text or to plain text. Charts come from [plot](/docs/libraries/plot). tuios draws every TML surface with loom.

The buffer belongs to the surface. The next `Render` reuses it. `buf.Cell(x, y)` gives one cell with its text, colours and attributes, for a host that composites cells itself.

## Flex layout

Layout works like flex boxes for cells. A `row` places its children left to right, and a `col` top to bottom.

- `w` and `h` take cells, a percentage, `fill` or `fill:N`. The `fill` boxes share the space that is left, by their weight N.
- `min-w`, `max-w`, `min-h` and `max-h` bound a size. A `fill` box at its maximum gives the rest to the others.
- `pad`, `gap`, `grow`, `align` and `justify` work as their names say.
- A box never draws outside its parent. When a column is too short, tables, lists, text, cards and tabs give rows back first.

A row that holds a button wraps when its children do not fit side by side. Whole buttons go to the next line, so no label is cut. A spacer goes with the child after it, so a hint at the right stays at the right.

## Colour depth

Every colour is a role, such as `success`, `mute` or `series2`. You give the surface a `Palette` at the depth of the terminal: `TrueColor`, `Color256`, `Color16` or `ASCII`.

- `DefaultPalette(depth)` gives the tuios palette at each depth. At 256 colours each colour is the nearest of the xterm 256, by distance in OKLab.
- `NewPalette(depth, roles)` builds a palette from your theme. A role you leave out takes a colour derived from the others. The pictures on these pages use `NewPalette` with the colours of this site.
- At 16 colours there are no grounds. The cursor row and a primary button use reverse video.
- In ASCII, loom draws its own marks in ASCII, and the charts use ASCII glyphs.

## Updates: the retained tree

A surface keeps its tree of boxes between frames. Nothing is laid out or drawn again unless something changed.

1. A state patch marks only the boxes whose bindings read the changed paths.
2. The next `Render` evaluates those boxes again, lays the tree out, and repaints only the boxes whose content or place changed.
3. `Damage` returns the rectangles repainted since its last call, including the renders that input caused. A host sends only those cells to the terminal.
4. A `Render` with nothing changed does no work.

```go
err := s.Patch(ops)          // a JSON Patch from the owner
err = s.Set("state.cpu", 43) // or one value
buf := s.Render(80, 24)
for _, r := range s.Damage() {
	// send the cells in r
}
```

This picture is the dashboard after a patch that sets `/passed` to 413 and `/failed` to 2. The tinted cells are the rectangles `Damage` returned. The chart and the table did not change, so loom did not draw them again.

<LibShot name="loom/damage" alt="The dashboard after the patch. The stats line, with 413 passed, 2 failed and the gauge at 99.5 percent, is tinted. The rest is not" />

On a 200-row table at 200 by 55 cells, a full render takes about 0.6 ms. The redraw after a patch to one binding takes about 15 µs. The tests hold both to a budget.

## Keys and the mouse

`Key` and `Mouse` take input. Tabs, sorting, the cursor, folding, scrolling, `set` and `toggle` run at once, with no round trip to the owner. `emit`, `close`, `copy`, `open` and `pane` come back to your program as `Effects`.

- A button's `key` works from anywhere on the surface.
- `tab` and `shift+tab` move the focus.
- The focused table or list takes the arrow keys, `enter`, and `s` to sort by the next sortable column. The same column again flips the order.
- A click opens a tab, sorts a column, moves the cursor, folds a card or presses a button. The wheel scrolls. A drag moves a split.

```go
res, err := s.Key("r")
for _, e := range res.Effects {
	if e.Action == "emit" {
		send(e.Name, e.Value) // to the owner of the surface
	}
}
res, err = s.Mouse(loom.Mouse{X: 25, Y: 0, Button: loom.MouseLeft, Action: loom.Press})
```

A click on a link comes back as a `link` effect. Your program asks the person, then opens it. loom writes no OSC 8 link unless you set `Options.Hyperlinks`.

## How do I

- **Draw at the colours of my theme?** Build a palette with `loom.NewPalette(loom.TrueColor, roles)`, where `roles` maps role names to colours.
- **Pick the chart glyphs?** Set `Options.Charts` to `plot.Braille`, `plot.Blocks` or `plot.ASCII`.
- **Keep a pie round?** Set `Options.CellAspect` to the height of a cell over its width on your terminal.
- **Show `ago()` values that change with time?** When `NeedsClock()` is true, call `Tick()` about once a minute. Only those boxes are evaluated again.
- **Draw the frame tuios puts around a popup?** Call `loom.Frame` with a title and an owner label, and draw the surface inside it.
- **Change the document and keep the state?** Call `Replace(doc, true)`.
- **Draw again after the theme changed?** Call `SetPalette`.

## Limits

- loom draws any tree, valid or not, at any size up to 4096 by 4096 cells, without a panic. Layout measures each box once per width, so deep nesting stays cheap.
- Every number that sizes something, such as `bins`, is clamped to its range. NaN and infinities take the default.
- It drops control characters at the cell.
- The store comes from `tml.StoreFor`, so a patch cannot grow `each`, `list` or the rows past the document limits.
- One frame has a budget of 1,000,000 expression steps. Each box gets an equal share: 1,000,000 divided by the most boxes the document can build, and at least 100 steps. The share depends only on the document, so a box shows the same values in a full render and when it is built again alone.

## Where it fits

- [tml](/docs/libraries/tml) gives loom the tree, the state store, the bindings and the actions.
- [plot](/docs/libraries/plot) draws the chart components.
- tuios uses loom for `tuios tml show` popups and panes. The daemon keeps each surface, and every attached client draws it.
- The pictures on these pages go from loom's cells to PNG through [shot](/docs/libraries/shot).

The package imports tml, plot, ultraviolet and `charmbracelet/x/ansi`. It imports no tuios package.

## Versions

The module is at v0. The API can change between minor versions. Its tags have the folder name as a prefix, such as `loom/v0.1.0`. The source is in the [loom folder](https://github.com/Gaurav-Gosain/tuios/tree/main/loom) of the tuios repository, under the MIT licence.
